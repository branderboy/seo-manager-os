# Pillar 1: Product correctness

Read before building anything new, and before agreeing to a feature request.

A capability does not get a contract until `PRODUCT_BRIEF.md` gives it all seven: user
problem, target user, user outcome, measurable success metric, defined scope, defined non
goal, decision owner.

If a user asks for a feature and the brief has none of that, the right first move is not to
start coding. It is to say which of the seven is missing and offer to draft the brief entry.
That is one short exchange and it prevents work nobody can evaluate later.

Questions the brief must answer:

- Who is the exact primary user?
- What costly, frustrating, risky, or slow process do they have today?
- What do they use instead right now?
- What outcome does the app improve?
- What is the smallest useful version?
- Why would they return, pay, refer, or retain?
- What must be true for this to count as successful?

Do not build a feature merely because it is technically possible. Do not invent a metric to
satisfy the template. An honest "we do not know yet, this is an assumption to validate" is
a valid entry and belongs in the assumptions table.
